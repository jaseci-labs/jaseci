- **`import from typing { Any }` is a hard error, and the repo writes `any`**: jac has its own `any`, and carrying `typing.Any` beside it meant two spellings for one type. They really were one type, and the checker says so: an `Any`-annotated probe and its lowercase twin produce a byte-identical diagnostic stream -- 15 `W1037` each, same messages -- across assignability in both directions, `isinstance` and `is None` narrowing, attribute and subscript and iteration erasure, `dict[str, _]` / `list[_]` / `Callable[..., _]` nesting, and unions. The one thing that told them apart was `W1104`, the advisory asking you to stop using one of them, so the advisory becomes the refusal. `E1122` fires at import resolution on every shape that binds the bare name -- plain, mid-list, and the `as` alias that `W1104` skipped outright -- and names the remedy in the message rather than only in the help; `W1104` is retired. Other `typing` names are untouched: `Optional`, `Callable`, `cast`, `TYPE_CHECKING` and the rest stay importable. Module-form `typing.Any` stays legal on purpose, as the escape hatch for reflection *over* annotations, which needs the runtime object rather than a type spelling -- `coerce_value`'s sentinel comparison and `NonGPT`'s eight fallbacks moved onto it, and `NonGPT` gained a real binding on the way, since its declaration never imported the name it used. 116 files migrated: 64 typing imports lose `Any` (the import goes entirely where it was the only name) and 1115 annotation sites become the keyword. One checker pin moved for a reason worth recording: `checker_narrowing_root_causes.jac` spelled a union member `Any` without importing it, so the union collapsed to Unknown and the attribute error it pinned had nothing to do with union handling; spelled `any`, the #7158 absorb finally runs and that error is gone, taking the fixture's total from 25 to 24.

- **Native: `codeinfo`'s pre-scan blocker is gone, and the hold on it is deliberate now**: `jac0core/codeinfo.jac`'s only `typing` import was `Any`, and it was the pinned pre-scan blocker keeping the module out of every native closure -- `scan_native_blockers` answered `unresolved import 'typing'`. With the import gone the scan answers `''`, and the three-item clearing list pinned against this moment is spent. Item 1, `BaseTransform.prog: JacProgram`, was already cleared by #8288's pass rim rather than by widening the field. Items 2 and 3 were the same glob twice: `CLIENT_RUNTIME_SOURCE_PATHS` would not lower as a bare `tuple`, and would not lower annotated either, because its rows were ragged -- two, three and six strings -- so a nested tuple annotation became one heterogeneous literal struct that could not store into a `%List.ptr`. It is a flat `list[str]` of `/`-joined relative paths now, rejoined with `os.sep` at its one use site. What the list never covered is the blast radius, and that is measured here rather than assumed: with all three answered, `codeinfo` does produce IR, but `unitree`'s closure goes from 11 members to 19 and picks up 21 un-waived seams. Only 13 are `codeinfo`'s own filesystem-and-config machinery; the rest arrive with `passes/uni_pass.jac` and `passes/transform.jac` and include `UniPass.transform`, `UniPass.traverse` and `UniPass.get_all_sub_nodes` -- pass machinery a parse reaches, which is precisely what must not ship as an `abort()` stub, and `unitree` is a live entry in `NATIVE_SEAL_ROOTS`. So inference stays held for `codeinfo`, by `PLACEMENT_INFERENCE_HELD` naming the module and the reason instead of by an accidental import refusal, and every sealed root's closure is unchanged. Clearing the hold is worth a wave: it was measured to take `capability_check_pass` from five own seams to one, three of them because it reads `codeinfo`'s tables and `ClientCapabilityCheckPass.transform` because `module_codegen_pass` joins alongside. `test_native_comptime_any.jac` is rewritten as the fix's coverage: the vocabulary question is closed permanently rather than deferred, because a name the language refuses can never reach the pre-scan.
