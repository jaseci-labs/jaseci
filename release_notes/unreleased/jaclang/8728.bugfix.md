- **Fix: the seal's cache-off intent survives a program hop**: `jac seal` compiles its closure with `no_ir_cache` so the demotion verdict comes from source rather than from whatever happens to be on disk, but that flag lived on the `CompileOptions` object handed to one `compile()` call, and a compile does not stay on one program. Anything the self-hosted compiler owns is routed by `selfhost.resolve` onto a long-lived `JacProgram` and compiled under that program's default options, where the flag is false. Seven of 114 dependency lookups in a `jac seal --compiler` run therefore answered from disk inside a seal that had asked for none of it, and which seven depended on what an earlier stage had left in the cache. Partial warmth is worse than either extreme: part of the closure is answered from source and part from disk, and the two disagree, which is why a refusal was not reproducible and why the seal compiled its closure twice after every refusal. The intent is now held for the whole compile region in `jaclang.compiler.driver.jir` and restored on the way out, so it reaches every hop and nothing leaks past the region. With the verdict reproducible, the unconditional retry is replaced by `seal_refusal_looks_like_order_miss`, which grants a second pass only to a refusal whose every entry is an E5092 cascade naming a callee nothing in the closure reported as demoted. Refusals no longer scrub the closure's cached IR either, so ordinary builds keep their warm cache. The demotion coverage now travels in the cache entry's meta, so a dependency served from disk reports its demotions instead of looking like a module that lowered in full. The abort-stub scan over the IR that ships is unchanged and still backstops the gate.
