- **Scale: revision based rollback and a bundle retention contract**: a deploy already leaves a complete rollback target in the cluster, since the pod image is constant across versions and deploy patches rather than recreates, but nothing exposed or protected it. `revision_history_limit` is now a declared `KubernetesConfig` field that lands on every Deployment, defaulting to the 10 the API already applied, so rollback depth is something a consumer can read back instead of inferring. `list_revisions` reports an app's releases, identified by the bundle digest, and `rollback_to` reverts every service of a release to whichever revision carried it, services first and the gateway last, waiting for the reverted fleet to come back before reporting success. It refuses when any service no longer has a retained revision, rather than half reverting an app onto two versions at once. The fleet waiter now holds until the Deployment controller has observed the patch and every replica runs the new template, instead of accepting any single ready replica, which a just patched Deployment still has from the outgoing one. Rollback restores code: a pod template is reverted, and the `[scale.secrets]` Secret it reads is always the current one, so a config-only redeploy is not a rollback target of its own. `bundle_keys_from_pod_spec` and `referenced_bundle_keys` give consumers the retention contract that a bundle referenced by a retained revision is never deleted, reading the keys back out of the init container the injector itself builds so the writer and the reader cannot drift.
