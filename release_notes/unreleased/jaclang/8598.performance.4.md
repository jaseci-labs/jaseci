- **Permission is decided by the query, not by discarding rows afterwards**: a gated read materialised every candidate and then dropped what the caller could not see. The permission test now compiles into the statement -- you own it (`root_id`), it is granted to everyone (`access.all`), it is granted to you by name (`access.roots`), or it is granted to a group you belong to (an `EXISTS` over your membership edges). Measured from a cold context: a gated read of 30 docs of which 3 are visible went from materialising 31 anchors to 3. Because the filter is now inside the query, a bound can be pushed under gating too, so `[:n]` keeps meaning "n rows you may see" while reading n rows instead of all of them. An archetype that overrides `__jac_access__` decides access with arbitrary Jac, which has no SQL form; those decline the pushdown and keep the object-space filter, as does any query with a non-persistent origin.
